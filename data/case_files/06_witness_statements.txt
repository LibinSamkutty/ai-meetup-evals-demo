Interviews conducted: 17 March 2026
Interviewer: Inspector Priya Menon, Special Investigations Unit
Location: MIFA Museum, Conference Room 2
All times in IST (UTC+5:30)

============================================================

STATEMENT — A.V. PAWAR (Night Watchman, MIFA Museum)
Time: 09:30 AM IST
Present: Inspector Menon, statement recorder

"I was doing my patrol rounds that night, same as every shift. My route takes me
past the east courtyard and the service entrance. Somewhere around 02:30 in the
morning — I don't watch the clock every minute — I noticed a grey vehicle parked
close to the service entrance door. It was a Bolero or similar, grey colour, and
it had an orange decorative sticker on the rear windscreen. I didn't see the
licence plate. I didn't think much of it — sometimes contractors and late deliveries
use that entrance.

I did not see anyone going in or out through the service door at that moment. I
continued on my patrol.

I want to correct something. In my patrol log note from earlier that night, I wrote
that I thought I saw Vikram Singh moving through the east corridor, around midnight.
I should not have written that. When I think about it carefully now, I was at a
distance in low light. I am not certain it was him. I cannot identify who it was.
I should not have assumed it was the Head of Security. I want that correction on record.

I did not see anything else unusual during my shift."

[Investigator note: Pawar's earlier patrol log note stating he saw 'Head of Security
Singh moving toward Gallery D around 00:30' was reviewed against CCTV records.
Security desk CCTV (Camera SD-01) confirms Singh was at his workstation at 00:02,
00:55, and 01:48 IST — inconsistent with movement through the east corridor in that
window. Pawar has formally retracted the identification. The retracted sighting is
consistent with mistaken identification in low-light conditions at distance.]

STATEMENT — VIKRAM SINGH (Head of Security, MIFA Museum)
Time: 11:00 AM IST
Present: Inspector Menon

"I was on my standard night shift, 22:00 to 06:00. I start every shift by logging
into the workstation and going through the overnight access exception reports and
the patrol schedule. That is my routine, and I follow it every night.

I was at the security desk almost the entire shift. I stepped away twice — once
around 23:20 for a bathroom break, maybe five minutes, and again around 01:30 for
another short break. Both brief. The rest of the night I was reviewing access logs,
incident reports from the north wing water sensor fault we'd been tracking, and
updating the patrol rota for the following month. The system logs every time I
interact with the workstation — you will see that in the check-in records.

I did not enter Gallery D that night. I did not use the Gallery D alarm override.
My Badge #0081 was on my person at all times. No one approached the security desk
that night to report anything unusual. The alarm board showed no alerts.

I have been at this museum for twelve years. I find it deeply troubling to be in
this room."

STATEMENT — MEERA JOSHI (External Art Appraiser, contractor)
Time: 14:00 PM IST
Present: Inspector Menon, personal counsel

"I have been working on the Nilgiri Taj appraisal since February. It has been a
significant piece of work — the painting is historically important and the valuation
requires careful comparable analysis from the period. I am close to completing the
formal report.

The door access on the 15th — the after-hours entry — I went back to collect my
appraisal notes. I had left a folder in the consultation annex off Gallery D.
I did not check the time when I went back in, but it was probably around 17:50.
I was out of the building by 18:10. I understand the system flagged it. I should
have kept better track of my access window.

The Belgian call — that was Johan van Brussel, a dealer I have worked with for many
years. He specialises in early South Asian painting from the colonial period, which
is directly relevant to a Pradhan 1903 appraisal. I needed final comparable auction
prices for a similar-period work to anchor my valuation. The call was entirely
professional. I can provide his contact details, the appraisal methodology notes,
and the comparable pricing data from that call.

Yes, I know my financial situation is known. The gallery closure was difficult and
I have outstanding debts. That is not something I hide. I came back to appraisal
work because it is what I am good at, and I am building things back. I did not
steal this painting. I have spent six weeks documenting its condition and its history.

The grey Bolero — my vehicle is registered MH02-AB-4321. I was home in Andheri by
20:00 on the 15th. The car was parked outside my building the entire night. You can
check with my building's security desk. They log overnight parking."
