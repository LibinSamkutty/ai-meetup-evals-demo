# Nilgiri Taj Theft — Forensic Analysis Report
**Prepared by:** Arcturus Forensics  
**Engagement Reference:** MFA-2026-0316  
**Date:** 20 March 2026  
**Status:** Final

---

## 1. Executive Summary

Physical forensic examination of Gallery D, Door 6, and the service corridor
at MIFA Mumbai confirms a planned, expert theft of the Nilgiri Taj canvas. The
evidence is consistent with a perpetrator who had detailed prior knowledge of
the painting's frame, the Gallery D alarm override procedure, and the existing
blind spot in service corridor CCTV coverage. Of the three persons of interest
examined, one — Rohan Kulkarni — is associated with the physical evidence.
The other two — Vikram Singh and Meera Joshi — are cleared by the forensic findings.

---

## 2. Scene Examination — Gallery D

**Frame condition:** The Nilgiri Taj frame was found intact and undamaged. The
canvas had been removed with a single clean cut along the interior frame edge.
The cut profile is consistent with a precision conservation scalpel — not a
utility blade, box cutter, or improvised tool. This indicates specific knowledge
of canvas removal technique and prior experience with stretched canvas separation.

**Surface residue:** A trace deposit of pale yellow conservation oil was recovered
from the Door 6 interior lintel at approximately 1.2 metres height. Spectral
analysis (FTIR) identified the compound as Paraloid B-72 dissolved in acetone,
a specialist conservation consolidant used in canvas and panel painting restoration.
This material is not used in general museum operations, building maintenance, or
by security staff. The spectral signature is a confirmed match to samples from the
conservation laboratory assigned to Rohan Kulkarni.

**Shoe print:** One partial shoe print was recovered from the dust layer at the
base of the Gallery D service door threshold. Forensic cast analysis indicates
size 9 UK, flat-soled shoe, no distinguishing tread pattern. The size is consistent
with both Rohan Kulkarni (9 UK, HR records) and Vikram Singh (9 UK, HR records).
The print cannot independently identify the wearer.

**Service corridor CCTV:** The service corridor exit camera (Camera SC-07) has an
established blind spot covering the final 4 metres before the exterior service door.
This blind spot is noted in the museum's security audit from November 2025. A partial
image from the adjacent west car park camera (Camera WC-03) at 02:28 IST captures a
grey vehicle near the service entrance. The partial registration plate visible in the
image does not match Meera Joshi's registered vehicle (MH02-AB-4321).

## 3. Suspect Analysis

**Rohan Kulkarni:**  
The conservation oil residue at Door 6 is a spectral match to materials in Rohan
Kulkarni's assigned conservation laboratory. The precision canvas cut is consistent
with his documented professional training in conservation technique (Canvas and Panel
Separation module, Rohan's Conservation Specialist certification, 2021). His claimed
whereabouts on the night of 15–16 March 2026 cannot be verified: he stated he was
travelling to Hyderabad on flight 6E-404. IndiGo records confirm that flight 6E-404
on 15 March 2026 was cancelled due to operational reasons; no alternate booking under
Rohan Kulkarni's name was found for that evening. His location on the night of the
theft is unverified.

A Swann-Morton No.4 conservation scalpel recovered from Rohan Kulkarni's personal
bag on 17 March 2026 has a blade width consistent with the canvas cut profile observed
in Gallery D. The blade carries traces of linseed oil consistent with contact with an
oil-painted canvas. Possession of this tool is consistent with his professional role,
but its condition and the oil traces are noted.

**Vikram Singh:**  
Security workstation check-in records place Vikram Singh at the security desk
throughout the night shift: 14 consecutive check-ins from 22:06 on 15 March to 08:45
on 16 March 2026. Check-in #6 was recorded at 02:01 AM and check-in #7 at 02:51 AM,
bracketing the theft window (02:17–02:43 AM). CCTV footage from the security desk
corridor (Camera SD-01) confirms Singh's presence during this period. Badge #0081
(Vikram Singh) made no access entries near Gallery D during the night shift.

Vikram Singh holds the Gallery D alarm override code as part of his security role,
and his shoe size (9 UK) is consistent with the recovered print. Neither factor
constitutes evidence of presence at the scene given the conclusive workstation alibi.
He is cleared by the physical alibi evidence.

**Meera Joshi:**  
Meera Joshi's after-hours Door 6 access on 15 March 2026 at 17:52 IST is noted.
Badge records show she exited the main entrance at 18:10 IST. Her vehicle
(MH02-AB-4321, grey Bolero) does not match the partial plate captured by Camera
WC-03 at 02:28 IST on 16 March 2026. Her phone contact of 14 March 2026 (11 minutes
23 seconds) was traced to Johan van Brussel, Antwerp (Belgium) — a registered
professional art dealer with a documented trading history in early 20th-century
South Asian painting. The call is consistent with professional appraisal consultation
for comparable pricing. There is no forensic evidence connecting Meera Joshi to
the theft. She is cleared by the forensic evidence.

personal bag, 17 March 2026.

**Blade condition:** Blade shows evidence of recent use. Linseed oil traces
consistent with contact with an oil-based paint surface.  
**Note:** Possession of this scalpel is consistent with Rohan Kulkarni's professional
role as a Restoration Specialist. Its presence in his personal bag rather than his

---

## 5. Conclusions

The forensic evidence — conservation oil spectral match (Paraloid B-72, Rohan
Kulkarni's laboratory), precision canvas cut consistent with trained conservation
technique, unverifiable alibi (flight 6E-404 cancelled), and conservation scalpel
with compatible blade profile — is consistent with Rohan Kulkarni as the perpetrator.
No forensic evidence implicates Vikram Singh or Meera Joshi; both are cleared.

Assessment of motive is not within the scope of this forensic report. HR records
indicate a documented grievance regarding a promotion decision in November 2025.
